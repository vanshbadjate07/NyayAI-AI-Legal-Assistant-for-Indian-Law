EXPECTED (reference answer from the labelled set)
    Legal domain  : rti
    Source Act(s) : rti_act_2005_en.pdf
    Section(s)    : 6, 7
    Must mention  : Public Information Officer, thirty days

GENERATED ANSWER
    To file an RTI application, a person must make a request in writing or
    through electronic means in English or Hindi or in the official language of
    the area in which the application is being made, accompanying such fee as
    may be prescribed, to the Central Public Information Officer or State Public
    Information Officer, as the case may be, of the concerned public authority.
    (The Right to Information Act, 2005, Section 6)

    The Public Information Officer, as the case may be, on receipt of a request
    under Section 6, shall, as expeditiously as possible, and in any case within
    thirty days of the receipt of the request, either provide the information on
    payment of such fee as may be prescribed or reject the request for any of
    the reasons specified in Sections 8 and 9. (The Right to Information Act,
    2005, Section 7)

    Therefore, the Public Information Officer has to reply within thirty days of
    the receipt of the request. If the Public Information Officer is unable to
    provide the information within this period, they must reject the request and
    provide a reason for the rejection, as specified in Sections 8 and 9 of the
    RTI Act, 2005.

    premises can be found in Section 11 of the Model Tenancy Act, 2021.

GENERATED ANSWER
    Under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, you can seek
    protection and relief from your husband's family's harassment. Here are the
    steps you can take:

    First, you should report the harassment to a Protection Officer. You can do
    this by presenting an application to the Magistrate seeking one or more
    reliefs under this Act. Before the Magistrate passes any order, you should
    take into consideration any domestic incident report received by him from
    the Protection Officer or the service provider. You can contact a Protection
    Officer who will ensure that you are medically examined and forward a copy
    of the medical report to the police station and the Magistrate having
    jurisdiction in the area where the domestic violence is alleged to have
    taken place (Section 12 of the Act).

    Additionally, you can seek a protection order from the Magistrate. The
    Magistrate may pass a protection order in favor of the aggrieved person
    prohibiting the respondent from committing any act of domestic violence,
    aiding or abetting in the commission of acts of domestic violence, entering
    the place of employment of the aggrieved person, or attempting to
    communicate with the aggrieved person (Section 18 of the Act).

    Furthermore, you can seek the help of a service provider. The service
    provider can ensure that you are provided with shelter in a shelter home if
    you require it and forward a report of the lodging of the aggrieved person
    in the shelter home to the police station within the local limits of which
    the domestic violence took place (Section 10 of the Act).

    Lastly, you can seek legal aid from a lawyer or a local legal aid clinic if
    you need further assistance or if you believe that your rights under the Act
    have been violated. It is important to consult with a legal expert to
    understand your rights and the steps you can take to protect yourself from
    further harassment (Section 9 of the Act).

    Remember, you have the right to seek protection and relief under the
    Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005. It is advisable to
    consult a lawyer or a local legal aid clinic for further guidance and
    support.

QUESTION
    The police are refusing to register my FIR. What are my legal options?

EXPECTED (reference answer from the labelled set)
    Legal domain  : criminal
    Source Act(s) : bnss_2023.pdf
    Section(s)    : 173, 175
    Must mention  : Magistrate

GENERATED ANSWER
    The police are refusing to register your FIR. Your legal options, based on
    the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023, are as follows:

    1. **Right to Information**: You have the right to send the substance of
    your information, in writing and by post, to the Superintendent of Police
    concerned. If the Superintendent is satisfied that your information
    discloses the commission of a cognizable offence, they may either
    investigate the case themselves or direct an investigation to be made by any
    police officer subordinate to them (Section 172, The Bharatiya Nagarik
    Suraksha Sanhita, 2023).

    2. **Legal Action Against the Refusal**: If the Superintendent does not
    register your FIR, you can send this information to the Superintendent of
    Police. If the Superintendent is satisfied that your information discloses
    the commission of a cognizable offence, they may investigate the case. If
    they do not, you can then file a complaint with the Magistrate, alleging
    that the police have refused to register your FIR in contravention of the
    Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (Section 172, The Bharatiya Nagarik
    Suraksha Sanhita, 2023).

    3. **Legal Representation**: Consulting a lawyer or a local legal aid clinic
    can provide you with guidance on the best course of action and help you
    navigate the legal process effectively. They can also represent you in any
    legal proceedings that may arise.

    4. **Documentation**: Keep all communications and documents related to your
    complaint and the refusal to register your FIR. This documentation can be
    crucial in any legal proceedings.

    5. **Community Support**: Informing your community and seeking support from
    them can also be beneficial, as they may be able to provide additional
    support or witness statements that could strengthen your case.

    It is advisable to consult a lawyer or a local legal aid clinic to
    understand your specific situation and to receive personalized legal advice.
